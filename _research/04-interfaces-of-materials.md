Interfaces are critical to how materials perform in application — for example, they are often the limiting factor in battery performance. We study materials interfaces through computation to unravel the underlying mechanisms and develop strategies to improve them.

A recurring theme in our work is the Li-metal/garnet interface, where poor wetting and contact loss dominate cell impedance. We identified strategies — including ultrathin oxide coatings, surface chemistry modification, and artificial interlayers — that negate this interfacial impedance and enable stable Li-metal cycling against garnet electrolytes. <span class="citation">[*Nat. Mater.* 16, 572-579 (2017)](http://dx.doi.org/10.1038/nmat4821); [*J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 138, 12258-12262 (2016)](http://dx.doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6b06777); [*Adv. Mater.* 29, 1606042 (2017)](http://dx.doi.org/10.1002/adma.201606042); [*Sci. Adv.* 3, e1601659 (2017)](http://dx.doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1601659)</span> We've also reviewed how small amounts of liquid electrolyte or liquid-derived interphases ("liquid phase therapy") can be used to conformally reduce solid-state interface resistance. <span class="citation">[*Energy Storage Mater.* 24, 75-84 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ensm.2019.07.026)</span>
