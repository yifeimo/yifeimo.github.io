Materials with exceptional properties often involve novel, complex physical mechanisms, and we aim to unravel them at the atomistic level — including disordering, frustration, super-ionic conduction, and non-linear dynamics.

Fast ion diffusion in superionic conductors proceeds through concerted migration of multiple ions rather than isolated single-ion hops. <span class="citation">[*Nat. Commun.* 8, 15893 (2017)](http://dx.doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15893)</span> Using the Density of Atomistic States (DOAS), a framework we introduced to quantify disorder and frustration, we showed that frustration enhances diffusion by broadening and overlapping the energy levels of atomistic states. <span class="citation">[*Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 62, e202215544 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202215544)</span> In halide electrolytes, we found that the superionic transition itself is triggered by collective motion of the anion sublattice, not just the mobile Li-ion. <span class="citation">[*Nat. Chem.* 16, 1584-1591 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-024-01634-6)</span>

We also study complex electronic phenomena such as polaron formation and hopping, which govern mixed ionic-electronic conduction. <span class="citation">[*Phys. Rev. B* 85, 081105 (2012)](http://dx.doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.85.081105); [*Ionics* 24, 1139-1151 (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11581-017-2268-6); [*ACS Energy Lett.* 9, 5334-5340 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.4c02060)</span>
